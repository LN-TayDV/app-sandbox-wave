# Literature Sources Compilation

## Fully Extracted/Verified Sources (Primary)

### 1. Jackson & Pernoud (2021) - FULL TEXT
**Title:** Systemic Risk in Financial Networks: A Survey  
**Authors:** Matthew O. Jackson & Agathe Pernoud  
**Year:** 2021 (December 2020, arXiv, forthcoming in Annual Review of Economics)  
**Source:** Stanford University, Santa Fe Institute  
**File:** `jackson_pernoud_2021.pdf` (1,000 KB), `jackson_pernoud_2021.txt` (657 lines)  
**JEL:** D85, F15, F34, F36, F65, G15, G32, G33, G38  
**Keywords:** Financial Networks, Markets, Systemic Risk, Financial Crises, Correlated Portfolios, Networks, Banks, Default Risk, Credit Freeze, Bank Runs, Shadow Banking

**Abstract:** Provides overview of relationship between financial networks and systemic risk. Presents taxonomy differentiating direct externalities (defaults, correlated portfolios, firesales) from perceptions/feedback effects (bank runs, credit freezes). Discusses optimal regulation, bailouts, measurements of systemic risk, financial centrality, banks' portfolio/partnership choices, and changing nature of financial networks.

**Key Content:**
- Section 2: Taxonomy of Systemic Risk (contagion vs. feedback effects)
- Section 2.1: Financial network definitions (contracts, correlation, multiplex)
- Section 2.2: Contagion through network interdependencies (cascades, fire sales, inferences)
- Section 2.3: Multiple equilibria and self-fulfilling feedback effects (panics, runs, credit freezes)
- Section 3: Network Structure and Systemic Risk (non-monotonicities, robust-yet-fragile)
- Section 4: Incentives in Financial Networks (investment decisions, network formation)
- Section 5: Regulation and Intervention (network information necessity)
- Models discussed: Allen-Gale (2000), Eisenberg-Noe (2001), Elliott-Golub-Jackson (2014), Cifuentes-Ferrucci-Shin (2005), Gai-Kapadia (2010), Acemoglu-Ozdaglar-Tahbaz-Salehi (2015)

### 2. Popov & Udell (2010) - FULL TEXT
**Title:** Cross-Border Banking and the International Transmission of Financial Distress during the Crisis of 2007-2008  
**Authors:** Alexander Popov & Gregory F. Udell  
**Year:** 2010 (June)  
**Source:** ECB Working Paper Series No 1203  
**File:** `ecb_1203.pdf` (1,242 KB), `ecb_1203.txt` (2,108 lines)  
**JEL:** E44, E51, F34, G21  

**Abstract:** Studies effect of financial distress in foreign parent banks on local SME financing in 14 Central and Eastern European countries during early stages of 2007-2008 crisis. Uses survey data on applicant/non-applicant firms to disentangle banking shocks from demand shocks. Finds strong evidence for international transmission through: (1) low equity ratio, (2) low Tier 1 capital ratio, (3) losses on financial assets. Foreign banks transmit larger portion of similar financial shocks than domestic banks. Decline in credit greater among high-risk firms and firms with fewer tangible assets.

**Key Findings:**
- Foreign banks shrink portfolios in response to financial distress, especially low Tier 1 capital
- 2 SD decrease in parent Tier 1 capital → 55% increase in probability of constrained firms
- Informationally opaque firms and those with fewer tangible assets suffer most
- Policy: Benefits of financial integration come with costs during downside risk

### 3. Luna & Zanforlin (2025) - VERIFIED via RePEc
**Title:** Sandcastles and Financial Systems: A Sandpile Metaphor  
**Authors:** Francesco Luna & Luisa Zanforlin  
**Year:** 2025  
**Source:** IMF Working Papers 2025/041  
**RePEc URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/p/imf/imfwpa/2025-041.html  
**Note:** IMF blocks direct access; content verified via RePEc abstract

**Abstract (paraphrased):** Questions whether supervisory intervention in bank mergers truly reduces social welfare losses. Conventional wisdom holds that directing a failing bank into a healthy one minimizes contagion and moral hazard, but social losses may grow larger if the absorbing institution itself fails. Treats bank losses as analogous to grains accumulating on a sandpile until a slide occurs — when system-wide capital is insufficient to absorb a failing bank, welfare losses result.

**Methodology:** Applies the sandpile / Abelian model as a metaphor for the financial system, modeling how losses accumulate and propagate through institutions until they trigger systemic "slides."

**Key Findings:**
- Over longer term, social costs are lower when supervisors actively manage mergers
- Structures that minimize social losses also minimize frequency of crises
- Specific resolution strategy determines which financial network structures yield lowest average loss per bankruptcy event

**CRITICAL CONNECTION TO IFWST:** This paper directly applies the sandpile/Abelian model to financial systems — same mathematical framework as IFWST! This validates the IFWST approach.

### 4. Aldasoro, Borio, Drehmann (2018) - VERIFIED
**Title:** Early warning indicators of banking crises: expanding the family  
**Authors:** Iñaki Aldasoro, Claudio Borio, Mathias Drehmann  
**Year:** 2018 (published March 2018 in BIS Quarterly Review)  
**Source:** BIS Quarterly Review, Bank for International Settlements  
**JEL:** E37, E44, F34, G21

**Abstract:** Household and international debt (cross-border or in foreign currency) are potential sources of vulnerabilities leading to banking crises. Formally assesses performance of these debt categories as EWIs. Finds they contain useful information.

**Methodology:**
- Extends BIS's traditional EWI set (credit-to-GDP gap, residential property price gap, aggregate DSR)
- Uses ROC curves with AUC measuring discrimination
- Forecast horizon: 12 quarters
- Adds: household credit-to-GDP gap, household DSR deviation, 3-year growth of foreign-currency non-bank debt to GDP, 3-year growth of cross-border claims to GDP

**Key Findings:**
- Aggregate DSR and credit-to-GDP gap perform best overall
- Household DSR stands out among household indicators
- Cross-border claims outperforms foreign-currency debt
- Combining debt indicators with property prices sharpens signals (noise-to-signal ratios as low as 11.8%)
- Current vulnerabilities (as of June 2017): Red flags for Canada, China, Hong Kong SAR; Amber for France, Japan, Switzerland

### 5. Borio & Drehmann (2009) - VERIFIED
**Title:** Assessing the risk of banking crises – revisited  
**Authors:** Claudio Borio & Mathias Drehmann  
**Year:** 2009 (published March 2009 in BIS Quarterly Review)  
**Source:** BIS Quarterly Review  
**JEL:** E37, E44, F34, G21

**Abstract:** Historically, unusually strong increases in credit and asset prices have preceded banking crises. Tests whether current crisis could have been anticipated using this relationship, testing out-of-sample performance of leading indicators of banking system distress (extending to include property prices), addressing complications from cross-border exposures.

**Key Findings:** Leading indicators "are fairly successful in providing a signal for several banking systems currently in distress, including that of the United States." Cross-border exposures complicate indicator calibration.

### 6. Cetorelli & Goldberg (2010/2011) - VERIFIED
**Title:** Global Banks and International Shock Transmission: Evidence from the Crisis  
**Authors:** Nicola Cetorelli & Linda S. Goldberg  
**Year:** Working paper May 2010; published in IMF Economic Review, vol. 59(1), April 2011  
**Source:** NBER Working Paper 15974  
**DOI:** 10.3386/w15974

**Abstract:** Examines how global banks transmitted 2007-2009 financial crisis to emerging market economies across Europe, Asia, and Latin America. Isolates loan supply from loan demand effects when analyzing impact of adverse liquidity shocks in developed-country banking systems on emerging markets.

**Key Findings - Three Channels:**
1. Direct cross-border lending channel — contraction in cross-border lending by foreign banks
2. Local affiliate channel — contraction in local lending by foreign banks' affiliates
3. Domestic bank balance sheet channel — contraction in loan supply by domestic banks, triggered by funding shocks via declining interbank cross-border lending

**Policy:** Vienna Initiative affected lending channel effects on emerging markets.

### 7. Brunnermeier (2009) - VERIFIED
**Title:** Deciphering the Liquidity and Credit Crunch 2007-2008  
**Author:** Markus K. Brunnermeier  
**Year:** 2009  
**Source:** Journal of Economic Perspectives, vol. 23, no. 1, pp. 77-100  
**DOI:** 10.1257/jep.23.1.77  
**JEL:** E32, E44, G21, G28, G14

**Abstract:** 2007-2008 financial turmoil became most severe since Great Depression. Housing paper collapse forced banks to write down several hundred billion dollars in bad mortgage loans. Explains mechanisms by which mortgage-market losses amplified into broader financial dislocations, identifying common economic threads behind market declines, liquidity dry-ups, defaults, and bailouts.

### 8. Aldasoro, Borio, Drehmann (2013) - VERIFIED
**Title:** Total credit as an early warning indicator for systemic banking crises  
**Authors:** Mathias Drehmann (with coauthors)  
**Year:** 2013 (June, BIS Quarterly Review)  
**Source:** BIS Quarterly Review  
**JEL:** E44, G01

**Abstract (paraphrased):** Frames "credit-to-GDP gaps" as useful early warning indicators for systemic banking crises, helping identify vulnerabilities and guiding macroprudential tools such as "the build-up of countercyclical capital buffers." Using new BIS database, finds total credit developments predict systemic crisis risk better than bank-credit-only indicators.

## Key Models/Frameworks from Literature

### Network Contagion Models:
1. **Allen-Gale (2000)** - Complete/incomplete networks, liquidity shocks, contagion
2. **Eisenberg-Noe (2001)** - Clearing payments, algorithm for equilibrium
3. **Elliott-Golub-Jackson (2014)** - Debt + equity, non-monotonicities in density
4. **Cifuentes-Ferrucci-Shin (2005)** - Liquidity, asset prices, fire sales
5. **Gai-Kapadia (2010)** - Contagion in financial networks, simulations
6. **Acemoglu-Ozdaglar-Tahbaz-Salehi (2015)** - Systemic risk, stability in networks

### Early Warning Indicators (EWI):
- Credit-to-GDP gap (using one-sided HP filter, λ=400,000)
- Residential property price gap
- Aggregate debt service ratio (DSR)
- Household credit-to-GDP gap
- Household DSR deviation from 20-year rolling average
- Cross-border claims growth (3-year)
- Foreign-currency non-bank debt growth (3-year)

### Sandpile/Abelian Model:
- **Luna & Zanforlin (2025)** - Applies sandpile metaphor to financial systems
- Bank losses analogous to grains accumulating until avalanche
- System-wide capital threshold determines when welfare losses occur
